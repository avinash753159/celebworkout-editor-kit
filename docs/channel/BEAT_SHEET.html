I. What this is, and who writes it

Gavia writes his in about two hours and calls it "a scene-by-scene outline of what should happen in the documentary." It is not a script. It is the document that tells the footage hunt what to go and find, and it is the highest-leverage two hours in the whole process.

The two-track rule

A feature beat sheet has one track. Ours has two. The story track carries the argument; the workout track delivers the programme movement by movement, each one attached to the beat that justifies it. Never one summary card — that is R10, and it is the single biggest failure of every previous script.

What Save the Cat caught that the other two missed

Finding — the B story

Save the Cat puts a B story at beat 7 — the secondary thread that carries the theme while the A story carries the plot. No Film School has no equivalent, and neither did our draft. In the Hemsworth film the B story is his father. Every previous version held Craig back until minute thirteen, which is why the ending felt unearned: the emotional thread was introduced after the audience had stopped being able to use it. It opens at 5:10 and runs underneath everything.

Finding — the midpoint is a false victory

Save the Cat's midpoint is a false win: things appear to succeed, and the stakes rise underneath. Our beat 10 — it worked, the photograph became the reference image for a decade — is exactly that shape, and naming it correctly changes how it is cut. It should feel like a peak and play like a warning.

Fields — every beat carries all seven
FieldRule
ARGUESOne sentence. What this beat claims — never what it says. If you can only write what happens, the beat is not earning its place.
TIMETarget duration. Sum must land 20 min ±1.
WORKOUTWhich movement(s) land here, or —.
PICTUREA brief a stranger could act on. This is what the Hunter receives.
TREATMENTPrescribed effect from the library. Never improvised.
FACTSWhich dossier claims this beat spends, with tier.
BITEWhat real voice is wanted here, if any.

V. The gate on this document

The Showrunner cannot hand off until

Every beat has an ARGUES sentence · durations sum to 20 min ±1 · the withheld promise is spoken before 0:20 · every movement of the programme is assigned to a beat · beat 9 lands before the halfway point · the B story opens in act one and pays in act three · every fact carries a tier.

The last three are what V1–V3 failed. The programme was never assigned to any beat because it was never found; the reveal sat at 90% of the way in with the workout promised and never delivered; and the father — the whole emotional engine — was introduced after the point at which the audience could still use him. All three are now checkable before a word of narration is written.
