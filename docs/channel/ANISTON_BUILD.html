The honest read on V13

Its own metadata file records a deliberate brand departure: the thumbnail carries no photograph and no likeness of Jennifer Aniston, because all four sources spent their one permitted clip inside the film, so a frame lifted from a bite would be a second use of that source. The dominant element is the film's own joint-skeleton figure over the red circular field. BRAND.md's "celebrity recognisable first" is not met. That is the single biggest open question on this film, and it is a thumbnail question, not a film question.

So: if you want this film out the door, the work is a watch-through, a thumbnail decision, and an upload. If you want to make it again properly — or make the next celebrity — everything below is the recipe.

The two parameters V13 breaks, and why it is defensible

Real voice and generated share both fail the Standard, and they fail together, for one reason: the copyright rule. One clip per source, ten seconds maximum, never repeated. Four sources buys you roughly forty seconds of her voice in a sixteen-minute film. Getting to 40% real voice needs forty-plus distinct sources — which is a sourcing project, not a picture decision. Until that pass is run, generated picture carries the film. Do not treat this as a bug to patch in the edit; it is a research budget.

The house rules that apply to every kind

Frame: full bleed. No inset, no stroke, no branded border. Burned-in captions bottom third, bold sans, all caps, speaker name as a hyphen prefix on bites.
Grade: one LUT across the whole film — cool shadows, warm skin, reduced background saturation, controlled contrast, mild grain. Archive is graded to match, never left as delivered.
Move: no shot is dead static. Minimum is a 1.02 breath over its full length, except where a row's own note calls for a true static hold.
Cut: every picture change lands on a narration boundary or a beat, never mid-word.

The disclosure that must survive every edit

The weekly schedule is an Aniston-inspired synthesis assembled from modalities she has publicly described. It is not her programme, and nobody outside her training has that. V13 says this on screen at WO-12 and again in the description. No calorie target, macro split, meal plan or fasting prescription appears anywhere. Nothing frames the method as treatment or a cure. The film hands you a structure; it cannot look at your back.

The research dossier holds three named on-record trainers — Mandy Ingber (Yogalosophy, documented since at least 2006), Dani Coleman (low-impact resistance, from 2021), and Leyon Azubuike (boxing/HIIT, Gloveworx). Two further leads were investigated and killed: no named spin instructor and no named Pilates instructor tie to her. Do not resurrect them without a direct on-record source naming her specifically.

source_highlight — the exact numbers, because this one resolves a standing fight

A previous film was rejected four separate times for showing websites — "Again it's a website. I did not want this." — while the rules demand that a claim resting on a published source shows the actual page. This treatment satisfies both. Build to these numbers:

  • Scale — macro, not a page view. Only 5–8 words span the full frame width. Cap-height ≈ 12–14% of frame height. You are reading over someone's shoulder.
  • Focus — the highlighted line is the only sharp band. One line out is visibly soft; two lines out is heavily blurred. This single parameter is what stops it reading as a screenshot.
  • Highlight — hard-edged rectangle, not a hand-drawn swipe. Top at ascender, bottom just under the baseline, overhanging ~0.15em each side. Desaturated warm red, semi-opaque, multiply blend — paper texture stays visible through it, black text fully legible on top.
  • Ground — warm off-white paper with visible fibre. Never pure white, never a UI background.
  • Geometry — baseline off-horizontal by 0.5–1.5°, plus a very slow drift. It must read as a physical page that was photographed.
  • Cut pattern — two highlighted claims that contradict each other, then a hard cut to footage of the person. The effect stages an argument, then puts a face on it.

1.8 · Audio

Four bugs are baked into this architecture. Every one of them shipped once. Never regress them.

  1. amix renormalises when a short input ends → clipping. Use normalize=0.
  2. Mono SFX + stereo VO in amix → narration comes out +3 dB hot. aformat both to stereo first.
  3. Never slice narration per beat. Per-beat AAC re-encode puts an audible glitch at every join. Narration plays continuously between interludes.
  4. Never acrossfade audio chunks. Each crossfade overlaps its inputs, shortening total audio ~0.3s per junction → cumulative A/V drift. Butt-join with 30 ms edge fades.

Keep the hard sync gate: refuse to mux if |video_dur − audio_dur| > 0.25s.

1.9 · Copyright arithmetic — the rule that shapes the script

Three rules, stated by the owner verbatim, twice. They are not tradeable against production convenience, and rule 3 is the one that gets rationalised away.

  1. No clip longer than 10 seconds.
  2. Never repeat a clip.
  3. One clip per source video — not one per scene. If a 90-minute podcast yields a perfect moment, take that moment and nothing else from that video.

Rule 3 applies to b-roll and background footage exactly as it applies to soundbites. A "background clip" is a clip. Which produces this arithmetic, and there is no other lever:

Real footage wantedDistinct source videos required
60 s6+
120 s12+
300 s (5 min)30+
600 s (10 min)60+

Why: Content ID matches against a reference file. Ten separate ten-second clips from ten different sources present ten trivial matches. Ten ten-second clips from one source present a hundred-second match against one rights holder. Duration against a single reference is the risk, not total screen time.

What does not consume the budget: print and document sources rendered as source_highlight or headline_over (unlimited — the cheapest way to raise the evidence ratio) · our own generated picture (costs money, not rights) · licensed stock and CC-BY music used per licence · brand graphics rendered locally.

V13's four sources, each credited on screen and in the description, each used once: The Drew Barrymore Show · Allure · Harper's Bazaar UK · a Jennifer Aniston / Reese Witherspoon joint interview. Two further quotations appear as on-screen document cards rather than footage, which costs nothing against the budget.

2 · The prompt library

Prompts are the film. Not a step in making it — the thing itself. An audit of the MrBeast film graded 88 KEEP / 20 WEAK / 10 FAIL, and the failures were systemic rather than isolated. Three principles govern everything below.

The three principles

1 · Write against the argument, not the sentence. Before any prompt is written, ask: what does this paragraph claim, and does this image advance that claim? The failure mode is keyword-triggered literalism — the line says "cling", a shot of clinging appears.

2 · Show the state, not a symbol of it. The viewer gets one read of a 4–12 second image. Literal beats metaphorical. A shot that needs explaining has failed.

3 · Wording is the only negative prompt you have. The Gemini Developer API rejects negative_prompt — along with seed, person_generation, fps, enhance_prompt, generate_audio and six others. It raises ValueError client-side before any request is sent, so these cost $0 but look exactly like the API being broken. Only aspect_ratio, duration_seconds, resolution, number_of_videos, last_frame, reference_images and http_options are accepted. No seed means no reproducibility: a rejected shot can only be re-rolled, never refined. Anti-text and anti-logo defence lives entirely in the prompt's own wording plus post-hoc QC.

2.2 · The seven agent prompts

Four agents, three critics. Each agent is the previous agent's editor — nobody signs off on their own work. The Showrunner writes the structure; the Cutter writes the words. Conflating those two passes is exactly how V1–V3 produced sixty polished narration blocks built on footage that did not exist.

01 · Archivist — the dossier

You are the Archivist on a Celeb Workout film about <NAME>.

Produce dossier/<name>/WORKOUT.md and SOURCES_FACTS.md.

FIND THE TRAINER FIRST, THEN RESEARCH THE TRAINER. They talk more freely than
the celebrity does, and it is what cracked the Hemsworth project. Check for an
owned fitness platform, published books, DVDs, or a company.

Fan out by life phase: childhood, breakout, peak, crisis, now.

Every fact carries a TIER and a source:
  CONFIRMED  primary source, named outlet, date, and you have read it
  REPORTED   secondary coverage, or outlets that disagree
  RUMOUR     tabloid or unattributed. Usable ONLY as critique, never as claim

For every trainer lead you investigate and reject, write a KILLED entry saying
who, why, and what evidence would revive it. A killed lead with no reasoning
gets resurrected by the next pass.

Where outlets disagree on a duration or a date, do not average them and do not
pick one. State the disagreement and give the on-screen wording that is
defensible: "documented since at least <year>, credibly older."

GATE: you cannot hand off until the training programme is named movement by
movement. Not "she does yoga" - the named poses, the sets, the reps, and one
coaching cue per movement that makes it work.

Then adversarially re-check your own verbatim quotes. On the Aniston pass,
8 of 11 verbatim quotes could not be independently re-confirmed and one was
actively wrong - attributed to the wrong outlet entirely. Any quote that
survives only on your first search becomes narrator paraphrase, and you say so
in the note field.

02 · Showrunner — the beat sheet

You are the Showrunner. You have the Archivist's dossier. You write STRUCTURE,
not words. This is ~2 minutes of skeleton, not a script.

Produce docs/BEAT_SHEET_<NAME>.html - 16 beats mapped to Save the Cat.

For each beat give:
  ARGUES   what the beat claims. Never what it says. If you find yourself
           writing dialogue, stop - that is the Cutter's job.
  TIME     target duration and row count
  PICTURE  the sequence of shot kinds, in order
  BITE     how many, from which named sources, or "zero by design" plus why
  FACTS    the tiered facts this beat rests on

Fixed requirements:
  - Cold open is the five-part formula. Slot 3 is an explicit withheld
    promise, spoken aloud, before 0:40. Write the actual promise line.
  - Title follows the grammar: The [NOUN] That [VERB]ED [SUBJECT].
  - The four chapters (THE ROLE / THE WORKOUT / THE DIET / THE RESULTS) are
    fixed furniture. Map your 16 beats onto them.
  - The programme is delivered PROGRESSIVELY - each movement attached to the
    story beat that justifies it. If all the exercises land in one block, the
    structure is wrong.
  - The B story opens in act one and pays in act three, and beat 1 and beat 16
    are the same object or image. An opening image that does not return is
    just a nice shot.

Then apply R7 to your own work: for every beat, name what the narrator does
that the picture and the quote cannot - supply context, make a link, land a
judgement, or turn the story. A beat where the narrator only describes what is
on screen gets deleted, not rewritten.

03 · Hunter — the bite bank (run ×12, one per source lead)

You are a Hunter. You get ONE source lead. Find at most ONE usable clip in it,

  transcript     the exact words, verbatim

  burned_text    any watermark, bug, lower-third or caption in frame

Boundaries are the job:
  - A bite starts at the beginning of a thought and ends at the end of one.
    Never mid-sentence.
  - Interviewer QUESTIONS are allowed - they carry content and create
    structure. Interviewer filler ("uh", "right", "mm-hm") never is.
  - If the clean thought exceeds 10 seconds, do not trim it to fit. Report it
    as "document card candidate" - the quote becomes on-screen type instead,
    which costs nothing against the copyright budget.
  - If a second person is on camera and is not named in the film, the window
    is rejected. Reframe subject-left or subject-right only if the subject

You may not propose a second clip from this source. Not "it's only b-roll."
Not "the trailer is promotional." One source, one clip, ever.

04 · Cutter — the script and the shot plan

You are the Cutter. You write the words, and you write them AFTER the footage
is known. You have the beat sheet and the bite bank.

Produce the narration script and pipeline/shotlist_<name>.py.

NARRATION

  - Voice: confident and specific. "Here is how she trained for the role."

    adapted." Never "this secret guarantees" or "doctors don't want you to
    know" or "this one exercise created her entire physique."

  Above each block, write the ARGUMENT that block makes as a comment. Then
  write the shots under it and grade each one against that argument.

  Each row carries:
    kind      still | video | diagram | card | bite

Do not author timing and do not author cost. Both must be derived from the

still  The generated still — 62 of V13's 106 shots

The formula. Subject state → composition → light → colour → depth → explicit exclusions. In that order, one sentence, no line breaks. It must describe a state the sentence describes, not a symbol of it.

<framing: close and low / wide lens / symmetrical composition / overhead>,
<single named light source and its quality>,
<colour: cool grey / muted green and grey / warm off-white>,
<shallow depth of field | flat fluorescent>,
no people, mild grain,
<when the composition would naturally carry type: an explicit,

Words that must never appear in a prompt — a gate greps for them

Banned in frame: text · caption · subtitle · logo · watermark · sign reading · words
Banned subject: jennifer · aniston · celebrity · famous actress

The subject list is R13 enforced mechanically: you cannot ask an image model for the subject's likeness, so you cannot accidentally ship one. The in-frame list is subtler — writing "no text" puts the token text in the prompt, and diffusion models are unreliable at negation. Say it positively instead: "every surface completely blank with no lettering, labels or markings of any kind."

video  The Veo shot — 12 of 106, and each one earns it

The test, asked out loud for every candidate: does motion carry meaning a single frame cannot? Process, change over time, scale revealed by movement, organic motion, the film's biggest beats. Everything else is a still with a depth push-in, at one seventh the price and sharper.

Constraints that are not negotiable: duration must be exactly 4, 6 or 8 seconds — the model accepts nothing else, and a plan authoring 7.3s durations was caught in review. Veo attaches an AAC track that cannot be disabled; map video explicitly at assembly rather than relying on -shortest. Quota (429) is periodic and is not spent budget — the model stays reachable, so generate stills as a floor and let video upgrade over them.

<the same subject as the still it upgrades, named identically>,
<the change: X moving from state A to state B>,
<what else changes as a consequence>,
<camera: locked-off | slow push>,
<light, unchanged from the still>,
no people, <the resolution the motion arrives at>

The real V13 example — and note it is deliberately paired with sh003, the same tread belt, so the film returns to an established object rather than introducing a new one:

The same worn tread belt slowing from a steady roll to a complete standstill,
dust settling out of the air, the overhead lamp unchanged, locked-off camera,
cool grey light, no people, continuous motion resolving into stillness

"Continuous motion resolving into stillness" is the sentence doing the work. It tells the model where the clip ends, which is the single most common failure in generated video — a clip that is still moving at the out point cannot be cut cleanly.

The failure pattern that cost five shots on one film

"Objects moved by nothing visible." Five separate shots on the MrBeast film asked for something to move with no visible cause — a chair sliding, a page turning, a door opening. The model resolves the ambiguity by inventing a hand, which then trips R13 and the no-people rule. The operator rejected two by eye; the audit found the pattern. If a thing must move, name the force: wind, gravity, its own momentum winding down, water. If you cannot name the force, it is a still.

diagram  The exercise animation — not a prompt at all

This is the most important structural decision in the pipeline, and it is the opposite of prompting. pipeline/ja13_figure.py defines a 2D joint skeleton — nine joints, eight bones — with keyframed poses per movement. Its own docstring states the case:

"Deterministic geometry beats a generated demonstrator: correct form every render, no AI anatomy failures, no photographic person who is not the subject, and a rep-count change costs a re-render rather than a re-roll."

Four rules against R9 and R13 in one stroke. There is no stand-in performing what the narration attributes to her, because there is no person. Form is correct every time because it is coordinates, not a sample. And it is free.

The renderer (ja13_diagram.py) draws it on brand: two families only, figure in the left column at 3–44% of frame width, type in the right, and a red arc tracing the path the working joint travels — which is what makes the movement copyable from a single glance. The traced joint is chosen per focus area: glutes→hip, core→shoulder, legs→knee, back→shoulder, shoulders→wrist, mobility→wrist. Interpolation is sine ease-in-out, not linear, because "real movement accelerates and decelerates; linear interpolation reads as a machine and makes tempo impossible to copy."

So the "prompt" for a diagram is a data structure:

Movement(
    name  = "Dumbbell Row",
    sets  = "3",
    reps  = "10",
    cue   = "Hinge at the hips and pull the elbow back past the ribs.",
    focus = "back",                 # selects the traced joint
    keyframes = [
        Pose(joints={...start position, normalised 0..1, y down...}),
        Pose(joints={...end position...}),
    ],
)

A rep is a round trip: keyframes are traversed forward across the first half of the phase and reversed across the second, so the loop is seamless by construction rather than by crossfade.

card  Brand graphics — 19 of 106

Cards are drawn locally. Nothing in a card is sent to an image API. There is a non-obvious reason the card copy lives separately from the shot's prompt field: the two document cards must credit the rights holder by name on screen, and a real person's name in prompt trips the generated-subject gate. So render copy lives in a cards section of shots.json, and prompt carries only a short label.

That label is not decorative either. The cache key is a content fingerprint that deliberately excludes shot_id and block_id, both of which are positional — so four chapter cards that all carried an empty prompt would share one content address and one cached render. Every card gets a distinct label for this reason alone.

bite  Real footage — the only place a face appears

A bite's "prompt" is a search brief for a Hunter, given in §2.2. What matters at the shot-plan level is that a bite's duration is dictated by content, not by pacing — it is exactly as long as the sentence. Round 1 of the V13 plan applied the 4-second cutaway floor to bites, which forced two of the four out of the film as picture, including the cold open. Round 2 gave them back. The floor governs still, video and diagram only.

document  source_highlight — the free lever

Print and document sources do not consume the copyright budget at all. Unlimited. This is the cheapest way to raise the evidence ratio in a film that cannot get to 40% real voice, and every sourced claim in a Celeb Workout film uses it. Build to the seven measured parameters in §1.6 — the focus falloff is the one that matters, because it is what stops it reading as a screenshot.

2.4 · The audit prompt — run this before you spend a cent

audit() in ja13_shots.py is the mechanical half: it checks that no generated shot is still marked FAIL, that each prompt is longer than 20 characters, that no banned word appears, that needs_video and kind agree, that every diagram names a movement, and that no shot is attached to a block the script does not contain. The judgement half is a prompt:

Audit this shot plan against its own narration, row by row. You are looking
for pictures that do not advance the argument they sit under.

For each row I give you: the ARGUMENT of its block, the narration paragraph,
and the prompt.

Grade each KEEP / WEAK / FAIL.

  FAIL if any of these are true:
    - the image connects to one WORD in the sentence rather than to the
      paragraph's claim
    - the image contradicts its own line (three chairs under "room for
      exactly one")
    - the meaning IS a sequence of events, not a state, so it collapses to
      nothing as a still (a calendar igniting, a grid filling)
    - something moves with no visible cause, so the model will invent a hand
    - it needs the narrator to explain what you are looking at
    - a visible human would have to read as the subject and will not

  WEAK if it is decorative but not wrong, or if a stronger literal image of
  the same state exists. Say what that stronger image is.

Then step back from the individual rows and report PATTERNS. On the last film
this pass found that "objects moved by nothing visible" drove five separate
shots and that a quarter of the script was a sequence of events rather than an
image - neither of which was visible one row at a time.

For every FAIL, write the replacement prompt. For every row whose meaning IS
the change over time, mark it needs_video so it goes onto the expensive path
deliberately rather than by accident.

Output a table: shot_id, verdict, one-line reason, replacement prompt.

On the MrBeast film this graded 88 KEEP / 20 WEAK / 10 FAIL. The value was not the ten failures — the operator had already rejected two of them by eye. It was the patterns, which are invisible row by row.

On three.js — you are already doing this, and it is the right instinct

The argument from the Bilawal Sidhu conversation is that code-generated visuals beat generated video wherever accuracy matters: a diffusion model will make a beautiful, wrong picture of a wave, and a coded scene will make a correct one you can scrub. That is exactly the reasoning already written into ja13_figure.py's docstring for exercise form.

Where three.js would pay on this channel, in order of value:

  1. The exercise diagrams in 3D. The current figure is a 2D skeleton. A three.js rig gives you a real orbit around a movement, a plane change (which is the entire argument of the low-impact method — load through position and range, trained in more than one plane), and correct joint angles from any camera. Same determinism, same zero cost, far better orbit and layer_peel treatments.
  2. The muscle highlight. BRAND specifies the working muscle in #B22B1A on a neutral grey figure. In 2D that is a coloured bone; in three.js it is a shader on an actual mesh.
  3. Timeline and anatomy infographics — the "thirty years of high impact" argument, the force-through-the-joint explanation. These are Cleo-Abram-class explainer graphics that a video model cannot render truthfully.
  4. Render path. Keep the existing contract: three.js renders a numbered PNG sequence to frames exact frames at 1920×1080, ffmpeg encodes, the assembler never knows the difference. Do not introduce a live WebGL layer into the render pipeline.

Remotion is the other one worth a look, for cards and lower thirds specifically — the brand-consistency complaint ("it didn't seem like one brand theme") is a motion-design problem, and React components with locked-down tokens are a stronger guarantee than PIL calls scattered across renderers. The path-drawn camera trick (draw a trajectory on a still, hand it to a video model) is real and cheap, but it is a location tool — of limited use here, where the picture is objects and figures rather than places.

Money safety, for a worker that spends unattended

  • Charge the ledger when the API accepts the job — not at parse-success (which leaves spent at $0.00 so the cap never trips and the worker burns the whole backlog while printing spent $0.00), and not before the call (which books money a client-side ValueError never spent).
  • Circuit breaker: halt after 3 consecutive failures, plus a total-failure budget — a consecutive-streak breaker is starved by alternating fail/succeed.
  • Split submit from download. A failed download retries the download. Resubmitting buys the same video twice.
  • A file on disk is not proof of a usable clip. Quota-halted attempts leave truncated files indistinguishable from finished ones. Trust ledger.is_done(fingerprint), never path.exists().
  • The project key file beats an ambient env var. A stale GOOGLE_API_KEY from another project cost three runs here. Print which source the key came from, masked. The key goes in ?key=, never as an OAuth bearer — bearer returns API_KEY_SERVICE_BLOCKED, which reads exactly like a revoked key.
  • Cache on a content fingerprint, not the shot name. Shot names are positional; swap a clip and a name-keyed cache serves the stale render. A re-roll then costs $0.04 and the assembler stitches around it.
