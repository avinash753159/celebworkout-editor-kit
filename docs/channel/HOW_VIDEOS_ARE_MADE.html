The method

How a Celeb Workout film is made

A twenty-minute documentary in which the celebrity and their trainer speak for themselves, assembled by scripts, proven by re-transcription, narrated by ElevenLabs' Titan, and handed to an editor as a Premiere package. This is the process in its current form, with every file, tool and rule an AI would need to run it again.

Current owner standard: the Lamine Yamal V6 documentary

September 14th 2026: after approving V6, the owner asked to make this the normal way of producing future videos, retaining its effects and making each new film better. This section supersedes older conflicting style, generated-picture and narrator defaults elsewhere in this page. V6 is the starting quality baseline; its subject, statistics, runtime and exact shot count are not templates for other people.

The reusable skill is celeb-documentary. The machine-readable default is C:\Users\avina\ytceleb\video_defaults.json. This section also contains the baseline for builds instructed to use only this HTML guide.

  1. Story: one supported central question, a compelling real opening, connected chapters and an ending that pays off the opening. Use the supplied MrBeast documentary's concise, concrete narration style. Narration adds context instead of repeating the interview answer. Let the evidence determine runtime and chapter count.
  2. Picture: real moving footage is the backbone. Verify interview windows, use the subject's actual video for their words, and choose distinct relevant cutaways. Do not fall back automatically to generated stills, generic strangers, repeated source windows or frozen padding. Generated explanatory visuals may be used deliberately, without presenting them as archive evidence.
  3. Design: use V6's broad Archivo Regular/SemiBold typography, warm cream #FFEDD7, warm dark #100904, restrained orange #F07739, generous spacing, cream chapter side panels and large chapter numbers. The approved influences are Superpower on Godly and ORYZO on Refero Styles. Inspect specific references and adapt them coherently to each subject. The correct catalog is styles.refero.design.
  4. Effects: retain masked upward text reveals with staggered easing, source credits and translation badges, numbered process stages, verified metric callouts, restrained sound bars, smooth opacity blends, and paced closing phases. Use source-aware grading, motivated camera moves, speech-ducked score and selected sound accents. Apply effects where they serve the story, not every effect on every shot. The decorative orbit, spiral and concentric circles remain retired, as does the old acid-lime condensed title default.
  5. Voice and translation: use the current narration_defaults.json profile: ElevenLabs Brian, nPczCjzI2devNBz1zQrb, eleven_turbo_v2_5, stability 0.5, similarity 0.75, style 0, speaker boost on, speed 1.0. Keep voice settings consistent. For non-English interviews in an English film, preserve the real video and provide a faithful English question/answer dub with matching English subtitles and separate original-language audio. Independently check words, names, meaning and turn timing. No invented answers or celebrity voice clone is implied.
  6. Improvement: begin each film with an IMPROVEMENT_PLAN.md identifying the V6 strengths to preserve and concrete gains for the new story. Preview the opening, title and chapter treatment before the long render. Compare final storytelling, picture, readability, motion and sound against the plan and approved V6 examples. Record real improvements and remaining limits. A new palette or extra effects alone is not proof of a better film. Promote future shared defaults after owner approval, not automatically after an experiment.
  7. Two checks: verify the completed local MP4 and package, fix issues, then independently reopen the final files, preferably fresh downloads, for the second pass. Check full decode, format/frame totals, loudness/peak and speech alignment, source/claim/translation accuracy, graphic phases, subtitle placement, coverage and flagged freezes/duplicates, XML bounds, relative media paths, package hashes and ZIP integrity. Tie reports to final hashes. An unchanged encoded audio stream may inherit prior speech evidence in a graphics-only revision; do not claim repeated listening or ASR when it was not done.
  8. Delivery: finished MP4 plus matching editable Premiere XML ZIP, all media, graphic source code/specs, fonts and licenses, script/subtitles/chapters, source timecodes, claim and rights ledgers, credits and truthful QC. Preserve previous deliveries. Resolve a subject-specific authorized Drive folder rather than copying Lamine's folder ID, and verify remote size/hash/parent. Email after each check when those messages and the recipient are authorized for the current project; record blocked sends honestly. Never claim continuous playback, Premiere import, AE execution or phoneme-level lip sync unless actually verified.

In brief

The channel is Celeb Workout, at youtube.com/@CelebWorkout/videos. Each episode asks how one performer built a body for a role, and answers it mostly in that performer's own recorded words. The current process turns a name into four things: a 20–25 minute review MP4 with a complete stereo mix, a portable Adobe Premiere package with every clip on its own track, a set of ledgers that tie every claim and every frame to a dated source, and a verified upload to a shared Google Drive folder.

Nothing is generated except the narrator's voice, selected through the latest owner ruling in the narrator section. No stills are padded to fill runtime, no people are synthesised, and no clip is trusted until its cut has been transcribed a second time and its frames have been looked at. The whole build runs on one Windows machine with ffmpeg, Python and a CPU; the only paid input is the electricity.

The process has been through several rejected generations since July 2026. The one described here is what produced four accepted films in a row: Dita Von Teese V2, Zendaya V1, Matt Damon V1 and Priyanka Chopra V1. The lineage, and what each earlier generation got wrong, is set out near the end.

The object being made

Before the pipeline, the target. These are the properties every accepted film has had, as stated in the owner's request of September 8th 2026 (USER_REQUEST.txt) and the editorial feedback of the same day (EDITOR_FEEDBACK.md).

Format
1920×1080, 30 fps constant, 48 kHz stereo, H.264 review MP4. Sequence named <SUBJECT>_V<N>_MASTER.
Runtime
20–25 minutes, only where verified story and distinct footage support it. Padding is a defect, not a fix.
Opening
The celebrity speaking, immediately, on training, food or something relatable. Then a 30–45 second fast montage with dramatic, ducked music. No title card, no slideshow.
Picture
At least 90% real moving footage by runtime. Interviews, training, rehearsals, performances, behind-the-scenes. Stills only when editorially necessary. No shot, still or window used twice, including stills the publisher embedded inside their own video.
Voice
Subject and trainer carry the film in their own audio. A narrator bridges, dates claims and never restates a bite. Narration is the ElevenLabs voice Titan – Deep, Bold, and Powerful, the earlier default (superseded by the later MrBeast V12 reference ruling in the narrator section). Nobody is cloned.
Music
CC BY 4.0 tracks by Scott Buckley, ducked under all speech, credited in the description.
Graphics
Restrained, removable overlays over moving footage: a source label top-left, occasional lower-third titles, an animated three-phase closing (comment prompt, like, subscribe). Full-screen information cards were rejected and are gone.
Delivery
Review MP4 with the complete mix, plus a Premiere portable ZIP: XML, Media folder, separate picture / interview / narration / music / overlay tracks, HOW TO OPEN, script, ledgers, credits, QC. Uploaded to Drive and verified by size and MD5.
Honesty
The QC report names what was and was not checked. No claim of having watched, listened or imported into Premiere unless it happened.

Rules that govern every build

These are the standing rulings as of September 2026. Each one cost a rejected cut. Where an older document disagrees, the newer owner instruction wins, and the older text is kept only so nobody re-applies it from memory.

The subject leads

Every point that can be made in the celebrity's or trainer's own voice is. Narration sets up and connects. If the narrator adds nothing, cut the narrator.

A bite is a complete thought

Never cut mid-sentence, never include interviewer filler. Interviewer questions are allowed because they carry content. Keep the context that gives the sentence its meaning.

Real clip under real words

When the subject is quoted, the picture is that interview at that timecode. A quotation over unrelated footage is a defect.

Picture matches the argument

Show what the paragraph claims, not the keyword in the sentence. No weightlifting because it is a fitness channel. No stand-in performing what the narration attributes to the subject.

Nothing twice

No repeated shot, still or source window, and check for the same still embedded in different source videos. Changing a crop does not make a reused image new.

Date everything

A 2019 interview is a 2019 snapshot, not the current routine. Role preparation is not everyday life. Never invent a diet, a split, calories or a transformation.

Never generate a person who exists

No synthetic subject, no voice clone. The only generated audio is the narrator's built-in voice.

Content fingerprints, not names

Every cache is keyed on a hash of the content that produced it. Every stale-render disaster on this project came from a key that was a name or a position.

Look before you call it fixed

A number improving is not verification. Generate the contact sheet and read the frames. Sample the middle of a span, never the end.

A check that passes on no data is not a check

"File exists" does not mean current. An unmeasured window that returns "no cut" is a hole, not a pass. Ask what the check would do with nothing at all.

Say what was not done

Premiere import, continuous playback and listening are disclosed as unperformed when they were. Never claim to have watched something that was only measured.

The ask goes at the end only

Like, subscribe and a subject-specific comment prompt as an animated closing that resolves the film. Never in the opening, never mid-film.

Rights and copyright: two recorded positions

On August 3rd 2026 the owner set three binding rules, recorded in docs/COPYRIGHT_RULES.md: no clip over ten seconds, no clip repeated, one clip per source video, b-roll included. The only remedy for more footage under those rules is more distinct sources.

On September 8th 2026 the owner answered a direct question about longer excerpts for all three current subjects with "no usage restrictions, I can use all of it", recorded in RIGHTS_CONFIRMATION.json as a user assertion, not an independent licence. The current films are built under that decision, with complete interview thoughts that run well past ten seconds. Every package still measures itself against the August rules in COPYRIGHT_REVIEW.md so the position is explicit rather than forgotten. A new subject needs its own recorded rights answer before production; the previous confirmation does not extend automatically.

def speak(block_id, text, previous, following, out_dir):
    cfg = {"text": text, "voice": VOICE, "model": "eleven_multilingual_v2", "settings": SETTINGS}
    fp = hashlib.sha256(json.dumps(cfg, sort_keys=True).encode()).hexdigest()[:20]
    dst = out_dir / f"{block_id}_{fp}.mp3"
    if dst.exists():
        return dst                        # same words, same voice: never re-spend
    r = requests.post(f"https://api.elevenlabs.io/v1/text-to-speech/{VOICE}",
                      params={"output_format": "mp3_44100_128"},
                      headers={"xi-api-key": KEY, "Content-Type": "application/json"},
                      json={"text": text, "model_id": cfg["model"],
                            "previous_text": previous[-400:] or None,
                            "next_text": following[:400] or None,
                            "voice_settings": SETTINGS}, timeout=240)
    r.raise_for_status(); assert len(r.content) > 1000
    dst.write_bytes(r.content); return dst

Three checks around the call. Before: GET /v1/user/subscription and refuse to start if the remaining characters are fewer than the script needs. Before: GET /v1/voices and assert Titan's id is present, so a wrong account fails loudly instead of resolving to a different voice. After: run the same voice QC as any narrator, silence trim, frame pad, independent ASR against the script, and regenerate only the blocks that mis-say a name.

Endpoints used: text-to-speech/convert, voices, user/subscription, shared-voices for library search, models. Existing call sites in the repo: GENERATE_FINAL_VIDEO.py (sentence-chunked, resolves the voice by name), pipeline/ja_rebuild_audio.py (per-block with context, subscription check, three retries).

Three failure modes explain nearly every defect in that history: a cache key that was a name instead of content; a measurement mistaken for an observation; and a tool whose "no data" answer looked like a pass. The current scripts are shaped around not repeating them.

The prompt that starts a film

The name alone is enough. The trainer's name, an era to centre on, and a recorded rights answer make it faster. This is the shape that produced the last three films; hand it to an AI with this page attached.

Prompt

Make a Celeb Workout documentary about <NAME>. Read docs/HOW_VIDEOS_ARE_MADE.html, then the request and feedback files in the production-trio folder, then the Priyanka Chopra V1 project as the worked example. Use the trio helper scripts; parameterise, never hard-code a subject.

  1. Write BRIEF.md and project.json in work/<slug>/v1. Record the rights answer verbatim; if there is none, ask for it and research in the meantime.
  2. Discover, research, acquire, caption, transcribe. Find the trainer first. Tier every fact and date it.
  3. Write script.json opening on the subject's own speech, bites carrying the film, narration bridging. Budget bite-seconds against runtime before writing; if the subject carries under a third, go back to sources.
  4. Prove every bite: re-ASR the cut, read the contact sheet, check identity and burned-in graphics.
  5. Plan and conform picture with no window used twice and every window matched to the spoken argument.
  6. Narrate with the current Brian profile in narration_defaults.json once the script is locked, QC the voice against the script by ASR, regenerate any mis-said block.
  7. Build the timeline, render, verify, audit duplicates and black frames, read every contact page, fix, approve truthfully.
  8. Package the Premiere ZIP, upload both files to the subject's Drive folder, verify size and MD5, share links.

Gates: 20–25 minutes; ≥90% real moving footage; no repeated shot; every claim in the ledger; no generated person; complete stereo mix in the review MP4; unperformed checks disclosed.
