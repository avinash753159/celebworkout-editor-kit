The bottleneck was never the computer

Three proposals to speed up the pipeline — rented GPUs, cheaper models, and free Chinese video generation. Two of them solve a problem you do not have. The third is worth about two dollars, and it fixes the wrong complaint.

3 August 2026 Hemsworth film · 104 shots · 12 min 47 s Cost to date $4.16

The instinct behind all three questions is sound: the film took a night and it should have taken an hour. But before buying hardware it is worth asking where the night actually went, because the answer is unflattering and it is not the hardware's fault.

I. Where eight hours went

The Hemsworth film ran from 02:26 to 10:32 — eight hours and six minutes of wall clock. Of that, one hour and forty minutes was a computer doing work. The rest was an assistant sitting idle between your messages, because I do not run unless you send one.

A GPU cannot make an idle assistant work. Every hardware proposal below is competing for the 60-minute bar, not the 6-hour one.

The render argument — real, but small

The push-in is a per-pixel cv2.remap. Measured on this machine it costs 0.538 s per frame at 1920×1080, and the film is 18,420 frames. The same operation as a grid_sample on any of the cards above runs in single-digit milliseconds. Transfer is not a concern: I measured the payload at 67 MB of stills up and 61 MB of clips down.

† Including upload, download and boot on a warm snapshot. Compute itself is roughly two minutes; the wall clock is almost entirely logistics.

Verdict

For rendering alone, renting is slower than the free fix. Half-resolution warping beats the droplet on wall clock, costs nothing, and adds no moving parts. Do not rent a GPU to render.

The argument that actually justifies a GPU

Here is the part worth your attention. The reason the film is stills-with-fake-parallax is that real motion was priced out. Veo 3.1 Lite is $0.05 per second; 104 clips at five seconds is $26, against a $10 ceiling. So the pipeline generated still images and faked the movement — and "images after images" is precisely the complaint in your own edit rules.

LTX-2 generates a five-second clip in under thirty seconds on consumer silicon and is the only open model producing synced audio and video in one pass. Wan 2.2 renders humans better — faces, skin, hair — and carries an Apache 2.0 licence, which for a monetised channel is the least ambiguous position you can be in. It is also five times slower.

Verdict

Rent a GPU to buy motion you cannot otherwise afford, not to save render minutes. Roughly $2 turns 104 dead stills into 104 moving shots — and that addresses the actual criticism, which was never that the film took too long to make.

The only genuine downgrade available is the five prompt-writing agents, which currently inherit the session model. Moving them to Haiku saves perhaps three minutes and a few cents. It is worth doing and it will not be noticed.

Verdict

Model selection is not your bottleneck. But the buried version of this idea is the important one: the stage that failed had no model assigned at all. Nothing in the pipeline searched for, cut, and placed real footage of the subject — which is why a film about Chris Hemsworth contained no Chris Hemsworth. That is a missing stage, not a wrong model, and no amount of compute fixes it.

IV. Seedance 2.5 — is it free?

No, on three separate counts.

There is no API to call

ByteDance announced Seedance 2.5 in late June 2026 and it remains pre-release for API access as of last week. No price has been published. Volcano Engine is the expected channel but has not opened it. There is nothing to integrate.

When it does land, it looks more expensive, not less

The free tier cannot be automated

The free element is Dreamina's daily bonus credits — a web interface with manual prompting and manual download. It is a way to try the model, not a way to produce 104 shots. And seed.bytedance.com is a research showcase page, not a service.

Caution

ByteDance has publicly warned that resellers advertising "Seedance 2.5 access" ahead of official distribution should be treated as unverified. Several are already selling plans. Do not buy credits from any of them.

V. What I would actually do

  1. Let me run unattendedsaves ~6 h · $0

    A /loop re-invokes me on an interval so work continues while you sleep. This is the entire difference between eight hours and ninety minutes, and it is the thing that actually broke on Friday night. Nothing else on this list is within an order of magnitude.

  2. Add a real-footage stagefixes the film · $0

    The GMA and Men's Health downloads are sitting uncut on disk. The captions already mark a clean eleven-second thought about his fear of forgetting his wife and children. Trim it to ten seconds, place it, and the film contains its subject. No hardware involved.

  3. Warp at half resolutionsaves ~40 min · $0

    Measured at 3.4× faster. On a push-in you are magnifying the image anyway, so the visible loss is small. Skip the depth warp entirely on the 22 shots that are cards, titles and music beats — those are graphics and gain nothing from parallax.

  4. Disk pre-flightsaves ~25 min · $0

    Refuse to start below 20 GB free. Every segfault on Friday was paging file too small wearing a disguise.

  5. Then rent the GPU — for motion, not speed~$2 per film

    An RTX 6000 Ada running LTX-2 or Wan 2.2, once the four free items above are done. Build the image once, snapshot it, and every future film boots into it in two minutes. This is the step that changes what the videos look like rather than how long they take.

Four of the five best moves cost nothing. The one that costs money is worth doing for a reason nobody asked about.
