2 · Rule precedence

Why this exists

V7’s chat-show frame was not an oversight. It was a conflict — “use real archive of the subject” and “nobody but the subject on screen” both applied and disagreed, and it resolved by vibe. A stated order resolves it the same way every time, before the shot is chosen.

1

Factual accuracy

Nothing invented, nothing conflated, every claim tiered. A beautiful film that says something untrue is worse than no film.

→ The Men in Black circuit was presented as the Thor programme through three drafts.
2

Legal and ethical lines

No claim that a protocol prevents, treats or cures a disease. A risk marker is never a diagnosis. Rights cleared before a frame is cut.

→ APOE4 was nearly shown as a diagnosis; a CC BY-SA medical still was nearly used uncaptioned.
3

Identity

Nobody but the subject on screen, and no stand-in performing what the narration attributes to them. Beats coverage, always.

→ V7 put a chat-show sofa on screen because real archive outranked identity. It does not.
4

The contract with the viewer

They came for the programme. It is named, complete, and delivered before the halfway point. Beats story elegance.

→ V3 talked about the workout for eight minutes and never named an exercise.
5

Brand integrity

The frame, the LUT, the type, the prescribed treatment. Beats a nicer-looking one-off shot.

→ V7 ran three separate grades because individual shots were optimised, not the film.
6

Coverage

Having a picture for every line. Beats runtime, and loses to everything above it.

→ Four empty rooms exist in V7 because coverage was allowed to outrank quality.
7

Runtime

Twenty minutes. Real, but the last thing to protect - never pad to reach it.

→ V7 shipped 12:58 silently rather than reporting that it could not reach 20.

Read it as: when two rules collide, the higher number loses. Coverage loses to brand; brand loses to the viewer contract; everything loses to accuracy. Runtime is last on purpose — it is real, but padding to reach it breaks five rules above it.

IV. Twenty minutes, or it has failed

The standard

A film is made in twenty minutes of wall clock, start to finish. Not two hours. If a run takes longer, something below was done serially that should have run in parallel, or something was downloaded whole that should have been grabbed as a section. Find it and fix it — do not accept the longer number.

The frames that prove it

Do not do these

Do not cut every 2.6 secondsV7 has 258 shots and not one held past 11.9s. This is what “the visuals are very fragmented… just images after images” actually measures as. Fewer shots, held longer. Median 8–12s. A shot that earns 12 seconds beats three that earn four, and it is cheaper to produce.
Do not put anyone but the subject on screenA chat-show sofa is four rule-9 violations in one frame. If a clip contains another person, it is not usable - crop it, screen it, or drop it. Every REAL-V row here names its source and its timecode for exactly this reason.
Do not let the narrator explain the biteV7 does it three times: “my little brother calls me up…” then “his younger brother did not get the part.” Then again on Limitless. Then again on longevity and grip strength. Narration sets up, turns, or lands a judgement. It never paraphrases the man who just spoke.
V4 uses full bleed, not an inset frameGavia's actual films use full-bleed archive with source watermarks in corners. V4 replaces the inset frame with a consistent documentary grade and archive-first sourcing. Generated shots must match the surrounding archive in grade and grain.
Do not leave three grades in one filmV7 runs hot orange studio, cold desaturated interior, and warm contrasty interview, cut together. One LUT, and generated material match-graded to the archive either side of it.
Do not fill runtime with empty roomsFour near-identical vacant interiors is the generated-still filler pattern. Generation is capped at 15% and is only permitted where no real artifact could exist.
Do not leave network furniture in the full-bleed frameThe Avengers junket backdrop is visible with branding intact. Crop it (960:540:320:0) or play it inside a screen, where the bezel carries the credit.
Do not deliver 13 minutes and call it the film12:58 against a 20-minute standard is 35% short. Runtime is a gate, not an outcome.
Why none of this was caught

V7 was built before any of these were checkable. Every failure above now has a gate that runs on the rows in this document — and the suite is validated by the fact that it convicts V7 and both earlier rejected films. A gate suite that passes any of them is broken and does not ship.
