The lucky part

That highlight colour is almost exactly #B22B1A. The style is already on brand — grey, white, one red accent, no gradients or glow. It drops straight into the 70/20/10 ratio without modification.

II. Why animation, not footage

Filmed demonstrator3D animation
Rule 9 — stand-insA person on screen performing what the film attributes to the subject. This is the exact problem that got 20 stock clips withdrawn from the MrBeast cut.Not a person. No casting, no likeness, no ambiguity about who it is.
TeachingYou see the movement.You see the movement and which muscle is doing it. Footage cannot show that.
ReuseReshoot per film.Build once, use forever. A goblet squat is a goblet squat in every video.
ConsistencyDifferent demonstrator, lighting and room each time.Identical every film. This is what "one brand" means.
Cost per filmA shoot, or licensed stock per clip.Zero, after the library exists.
What this replaces

Nine rows in the Hemsworth shot list were pointing at MENSHEALTH.mp4 — footage of "Bob", a presenter Zocchi jokes about on camera because "we can't get him, mate, he's too expensive." Those rows are now ANIM. The Men's Health timecode stays on each row as the authority for what the movement is, not as the picture.

Film a demonstrator on whitea shootone dayReal, but reintroduces a person on screen and cannot show the muscle. Loses both advantages.
Generate with Flow / Veo~$0.25/5sminutesNo. Exercise form must be anatomically correct, and generated video cannot be trusted to get joint angles right. This is an educational fitness channel — wrong form is a safety problem, not a style problem.
The hard line

Generation never touches exercise demonstration. Everywhere else in the pipeline, a generated still is a cost-saving fallback. Here it is a liability: a viewer copying a hallucinated joint angle gets hurt. Animation is licensed or built, never generated.
