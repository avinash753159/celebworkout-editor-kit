II. Where the visual comes from — the real answer

You asked where the picture is. Here it is, and it explains “images after images” better than any note in the edit rules.

V3 has 80 PICTURE cues. Six of them are footage of Chris Hemsworth. The rest are instructions like “a consulting room. A page, face down on a desk. Nobody in frame”, “a search bar, mid-type”, “a calendar grid, productions overlapping”. Every one of those is a generated still, because nothing else can produce them. The script writes the picture department into a corner — it asks for images that exist nowhere, so the machine invents them, and inventions look invented.

The rule

A generated still is only allowed where no real artefact could exist. If the thing is real — a tweet, an article, a study, a set photo, a piece of equipment, a person speaking — the picture is the real thing, not a rendering of it. Generation is the floor under the pool, not the pool.

This is also why a workout guide is easier to picture than a documentary. An exercise is a real, filmable, demonstrable object. Every one of the eight movements is a card plus a demonstration plus a reason. That is 22% of the film that builds itself, on brand, at zero generation cost — and it is the 22% the viewer actually came for.

III. The braid

V3 segregates. Story block, workout block, story block — so half the audience is always waiting out the half they didn't come for. The fix comes straight out of the writing principles you sent: “every element does more than one thing… single-purpose prose makes fiction go flat.”

So the two tracks run together. Each movement of the programme is attached to the story beat that explains why it exists. Nothing is delivered as a summary card at minute four and nothing waits eleven minutes for its payoff.

The change

The reveal moves from 10:30 of an 11.6-minute film — 90% of the way in — to 10:00 of a 20-minute film, the exact midpoint. Not a smaller wait dressed up: the audience now reaches it already paid, with the whole programme in hand, and the back half is the story they stayed for rather than the thing they were made to wait through.

V. What has to happen before this can be written

  1. Find the eight movementsblocking · ~1 hour

    They are not in the dossier. Not one. Zocchi's published Centr programming, the Men's Health footage already on disk, and the set interviews are the places to look. Until this exists there is no guide, and V4 is V3 with better sentences.

  2. Acquire 10–15 sourcesblocking · sourcing team

    Six bites cannot carry 26 minutes at a 40% real-voice floor. Of the five sources in SOURCES.md, one is acquired. This is the stage SPEED_PLAN identified as missing entirely.

  3. Re-outline to the braidstory-planning

    Sixteen movements across two programmes, each pinned to the story beat that justifies it. Twenty minutes: the first programme fully delivered by 7:20, the reveal at the 10:00 midpoint, the second programme at 16:10.

  4. Draft V4, then reader-sim itreader-sim

  5. llm-writing passlanguage discipline

Bottom line

The script isn't broken because the prose is weak. It's broken because nobody ever found out what the workout was, and every other defect — the missing picture, the narrator doing all the work, the withheld reveal in the wrong place — grew in the space that left.
