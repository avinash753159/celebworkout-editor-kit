I. What a Celeb Workout film is

Hollywood performance, decoded into practical workouts and diet breakdowns. An entertainment documentary fused with a premium fitness magazine — not a gym channel.

Tagline: TRAIN LIKE THE ICONS · Personality: bold, cinematic, disciplined, aspirational.

The contract with the viewer

They came for the workout. They must leave with it — named, complete, and doable on Monday. The story is why they stay; the programme is why they clicked. A film that withholds the programme has broken the contract no matter how good the story is.

The cold open — a fixed five-part formula

Not a style. A template with slots. All three Gavia films analysed open identically.

#SlotExample
1Direct address to a specific real artifact. Never a biography, never a date."Pay attention to this little girl."
2The artifact is a genuine recorded document — a photo, a post, a capturea Street View frame from twelve years earlier
3An explicit withheld promise, spoken aloud, before 0:20"I'll reveal later what she whispered to him." — lands at 0:14
4Rapid cascade of archival voices, minimal narrationseveral sources, fast
5The central question, spoken aloud"Will he get to the top or self-destruct along the way?"

Title grammar

The [NOUN] That/Who [VERB]ED [SUBJECT] — a hidden cause plus its effect on a known subject, then the channel's search terms behind a pipe.

The Girl Who Broke Khamzat Chimaev · Why the UFC Broke Kimbo Slice · The Night that Ended Islam Makhachev's Career

Two writing jobs, two owners

The Showrunner writes the structure. The Cutter writes the words. They are different jobs and must not be the same pass — that conflation is exactly how V1–V3 produced sixty polished narration blocks built on footage that did not exist.

source_highlight — pinned from frames, not described

Measured off Johnny Harris @ 0:21, frames saved to dossier/references/johnny_harris_0021/. Build to these numbers.

Observed parameters
ParameterObserved
ScaleMacro, not a page view. Only 5–8 words span the full frame width. Cap-height ≈ 12–14% of frame height. You are reading over someone's shoulder, not looking at a document.
FocusThe highlighted line is the only sharp band in frame. One line above and below is visibly soft; two lines out is heavily blurred. This is what stops it reading as a screenshot.
Highlight shapeHard-edged rectangle. Not a hand-drawn swipe. Top at ascender, bottom just under the baseline, overhanging ~0.15em left and right of the phrase.
Highlight colourDesaturated warm red / coral, semi-opaque. Paper texture stays visible through it. Black text remains fully legible on top — multiply blend, never a covering fill.
GroundWarm off-white paper with visible fibre. Never pure white, never a UI background.
GeometryBaseline off-horizontal by roughly 0.5–1.5°, plus a very slow drift. It must read as a physical page that was photographed.
GradeVignette into the corners, light grain over the whole frame.
Cut patternTwo highlighted claims that contradict each other — "Oswald acted alone." then "shot from the front" — then hard cut to archival footage of the person. The effect is not decoration; it stages an argument and then puts a face on it.
Correction to the old catalogue FX_CATALOGUE.md filed this reference under annotate — circles and arrows drawn on in sequence — "awaiting screenshot to pin the exact treatment." The frames show something else. There are no circles, arrows or underlines at 0:21. It is a highlighted line on a macro-shot page, which is source_highlight. annotate keeps its slot but has no pinned reference and is built last.
Why the highlight effect matters most here The last film was rejected four times for showing websites — "Again it's a website. I did not want this." — while the rules demand that a claim resting on a published source shows the actual page. source_highlight is the resolution: it shows the source without looking like a screenshot. Every sourced claim in a Celeb Workout film uses it. No raw screenshots, ever.

All effects cost nothing per shot — they composite material already owned. Contract: effect(sources, frames, **params) -> Path, exactly frames frames at 1920×1080/24. The assembler refuses anything else. Word-level timings for text sync live in manifest/words.json.

VIII. Hard rules

R1 · owner: CutterA shot illustrates the argument of the paragraph, never a word in the sentence. If the image connects to only one word it is wrong even when beautiful.
R2 · owner: CutterFewer shots, held longer. Median 8–12 s.
R3 · owner: HunterA bite plays a complete thought or it is not used. Never cut mid-sentence. Interviewer questions are allowed — they carry content and create structure. Interviewer filler ("uh", "right", "mm-hm") never is.
R4 · owner: CutterNarration never restates a bite, before or after. It sets up, turns, or lands a judgement. Never summarise a bite after it plays — cut away.
R5 · owner: HunterNo single source exceeds 20% of total bite time. Draw on 10–15 sources so no original is a substitute for the film.
R6 · owner: CutterEvery frame carries the brand. Same red, same three families, same devices, same cards, every upload. Identity is cumulative.
R7 · owner: ShowrunnerIf the narrator adds nothing, cut the narrator. Every block must do something the picture and the quote cannot.
R8 · owner: ArchivistNothing invented and nothing conflated. Every on-screen claim is CONFIRMED or visibly labelled REPORTED. A magazine reconstruction is never CONFIRMED.
R9 · owner: HunterNo stand-ins. Nobody but the subject performs what the narration attributes to the subject.
R10 · owner: ShowrunnerThe programme is delivered progressively, movement by movement, each attached to the story beat that justifies it. Never one summary card.
R11 · owner: CutterSFX is sparse: room tone and at most one or two accents per film. Never wall-to-wall foley — silence is deployed, not filled.
R12 · owner: CutterEvery licensed music or SFX track is logged — source, licence, attribution text — before a film publishes. Scott Buckley requires on-screen/description attribution; nothing ships without it.
R13 · owner: CompositorNo generated faces, ever. A generated re-enactment shows anonymous figures, silhouettes, hands, or objects only — never a rendered likeness of the subject or anyone else. Real archive is the only legitimate source for a face on screen.

How to use this file

Every agent loads The Standard before it starts. Every critic scores against it. When a film ships, nothing in it should be traceable to a decision made outside this document — and when this document is wrong, it is edited here, once, for every film that follows.
