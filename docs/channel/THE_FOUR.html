System design · four agents · Celeb Workout

The Four

Gavia's four production stages, given identities, mandates, and the right to refuse each other's work. Every rule below exists because a specific rejected cut proved it was needed.

3 August 2026Input: a celebrity name. Output: a finished film.

I. The principle that makes it work

Four agents is easy. Four agents that catch each other is the design. The rule is simple and it comes from how real production works:

The rule

Each agent is the previous agent's editor. Nobody signs off on their own work. The Showrunner can reject the dossier. The Hunter can reject a beat. The Cutter can reject a bite. A refusal travels back up the chain, not sideways into a workaround.

That single rule is the fix for the worst failure in the project so far: SCRIPT_V3 demanded shots that exist nowhere on earth — "a consulting room. A page, face down on a desk. Nobody in frame" — and nothing in the pipeline was allowed to say no such footage exists. So the picture layer invented it, 84% of the film became generated stills, and you called it "images after images." The Hunter's right of refusal is that failure, designed out.

Agent 01
THE ARCHIVIST
Story research

Know more about this person than anyone who will ever watch the film.

Owns

  • Every fact, and the source under it
  • The life timeline, dated
  • The training programme — named, exercise by exercise
  • The candidate source list, transcript-scored

Does not own

What the story is. What's interesting. Anything about structure. It reports; it does not select.

InA name. Nothing else.
"Chris Hemsworth"
Outdossier.json — dated events with citations and a confidence tier · the programme · the trainer · owned platforms · ranked candidate sources

Signature move

Find the trainer, then research the trainer. Every famous physique has a named professional behind it, and the trainer talks far more freely than the celebrity does. This is the move that cracked Hemsworth: Zocchi's name → Centr → thirty named movements, after three script versions had found zero.

Must refuse

Any claim without a source. Every fact carries a tier — CONFIRMED (primary document or on-camera), REPORTED (press, single source), RUMOUR (never usable on screen). A magazine reconstruction is never CONFIRMED.

Gate before handoff

Blocks the Showrunner until≥40 sourced events · the training programme located and named movement by movement · ≥10 candidate sources with captions already pulled · every claim tiered

Prevents: a workout film with no workout in it. The Archivist cannot hand off until the programme is found, so V3's central hole is structurally impossible.

Agent 02
THE SHOWRUNNER
Beat sheet

Decide what the film is about, and what happens in what order.

Owns

  • The thesis — one sentence, the whole film
  • The beat sheet: 12–16 beats, scene by scene
  • The cold open, the title, the runtime shape
  • The brief handed to each Hunter

Does not own

Facts (the Archivist's). Prose (the Cutter's). What footage exists (the Hunter's). It works in two hours of thinking, not sixty blocks of writing — Gavia's beat sheet takes two hours because it is a skeleton, not a script.

Indossier.json
Outbeats.json — per beat: what it argues, the picture brief, target duration, which facts it spends

Rules baked in

  • Cold open is the fixed five-part formula — real artifact addressed directly, withheld promise spoken aloud by 0:20, archival voice cascade, central question stated
  • Title grammar: The [NOUN] That [VERB]ED [SUBJECT] — hidden cause, known subject
  • The programme is delivered progressively, never as one summary card. Each movement attaches to the story beat that justifies it
  • A beat states what it argues, never what it says

Must refuse

A beat that exists only because it is interesting. Every beat advances the thesis or it is cut. And it refuses the Archivist — sending the dossier back if the facts cannot carry a thesis, or the programme is missing.

Gate before handoff

Blocks the Hunter untilthesis in one sentence · 12–16 beats, each with a picture brief a stranger could act on · cold-open formula present · every movement of the programme assigned to a beat · runtime projection 20 min ±2

Prevents: "the story doesn't make sense." Structure is decided once, on paper, for pennies — instead of three times, after rendering.

Agent 03 · fans out 12×, one per beat
THE HUNTER
Video research

Find what actually exists. Never invent, never approximate, never accept a substitute quietly.

Owns

  • The bite bank — every usable clip, with source and timecode
  • Complete-thought boundaries, cut on word-level timings
  • Identity verification — is the subject actually in this frame
  • Per-source caps and clearance

Does not own

What the beats say. It does not rewrite the film — it reports what the world contains.

InOne beat brief. Twelve Hunters run at once, one per beat.
Outbitebank.json — per beat: real footage with source, timecode, thought boundaries, identity verdict · or a refusal

Signature move

Transcript-first. Never download a video to find out what is in it. Captions pull in ~200 ms with no download, so the Hunter searches two hundred videos and downloads the eight that matter. The last build downloaded 193 MB to discover a workout the captions revealed in two seconds.

The power of refusal — the most important line in this document

The Hunter can return NO FOOTAGE EXISTS and propose what does. That refusal goes back to the Showrunner and the beat is rewritten before anything is generated. This is the mechanism that stops a script demanding an empty consulting room and getting a hallucination instead.

Must also refuse

A bite that starts or ends mid-thought · interviewer filler ("uh", "right") · any source exceeding 20% of total bite time · any frame containing someone who is not the subject · a stand-in performing what the narration attributes to the subject

Gate before handoff

Blocks the Cutter until≥10 distinct sources · ≥8 min of usable bite time · no source over 20% · every bite a complete thought · every bite identity-verified

Prevents: "images after images" · a Hemsworth film with no Hemsworth in it · "so many clips taken from the same one."

Agent 04 · single, never parallel
THE CUTTER
Edit

Make the film. Every word written to footage that already exists.

Owns

  • The script — written after the footage is known, never before
  • Narration, the radio edit, music placement
  • Picture placement, shot length, effects
  • The render

Does not own

Facts, structure, or what exists. If the Cutter wants something that isn't in the bank, it asks the Hunter — it does not invent it. Runs alone. One intelligence holds the whole cut — this is the one stage Gavia refuses to delegate even to a full-time assistant editor, and the one stage that must not be parallelised.

Inbeats.json + bitebank.json
OutThe film. Plus the radio edit, gated before a cent of narration is bought.

Rules baked in

  • Narration sets up, turns, or lands a judgement — it never restates the bite beside it, before or after
  • Never summarise a bite after it plays. Cut away instead
  • Median hold 8–12 s, not 4–6
  • Picture serves the paragraph's argument, never a word in the sentence
  • A generated still only where no real artifact could exist

Must refuse

Its own narration, when it echoes an adjacent bite. And it refuses the Hunter — sending back bites that will not cut, or a bank too thin to reach 40% real voice.

Gate before release

Ships only whenreal voice ≥40% · zero echo · median hold 8–12 s · every movement named on screen · generated share ≤15% · runtime 20 min ±1 · brand sampled · and the three critic lenses pass twice running

Prevents: "the narrator says it, then he says the exact same thing" · "he hasn't finished his sentence" · "it was just cut and paste."

III. The three critics

Not one reviewer saying yes. Three lenses that disagree, majority convicts, and a verdict routes back to the agent that caused the defect — not to you.

How they earn trust

The critics are not graded on whether they run. They are graded on whether they convict the two films you already rejected — MRBEAST_V6 and THE_BODY_HE_STOPPED_BUILDING — for the reasons you gave in the voice note. A critic panel that passes either one is broken and does not ship.

The Fact-Checker's first catch is already known: SCRIPT_V3 presents the Men in Black dumbbell circuit as the Thor programme. Zocchi says on camera that the look was "more athletic, less bulky than Thor" and calls it "the man in black workout." That error survived three drafts, a full render, and a five-way review.
