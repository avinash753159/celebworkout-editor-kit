# Copyright review — all films

The owner's three binding clip rules (`docs/COPYRIGHT_RULES.md`, 3 August 2026):

1. No clip longer than 10 seconds.
2. Do not repeat the same clip.
3. **Only one clip per source video** — b-roll counts as a clip.

Rule 3 is arithmetic: a source video yields one clip of at most ten
seconds, ever. More real footage is only ever bought with **more distinct
source videos** — 5 minutes needs 30+ sources. Shortening or re-ordering
cuts from the same source does not help.

## How to read this

**Zendaya, Matt Damon, Priyanka, Dita** were built after the owner recorded a
footage waiver on 2026-09-08 — *"User confirmed all footage, longer excerpts,
no usage restrictions"*. Their numbers sit outside the three rules by a
recorded decision, not by accident. The measurement is printed so the position
is explicit. That waiver was a rights assertion by the owner; it was never
independently certified, and Content ID does not read waivers.

**Jennifer Aniston V13** is the only film that meets all three rules as
written: four interview bites, four distinct sources, longest 7.6s. Its
picture is otherwise AI-generated stills, cards, diagrams and motion, which
consume no third-party budget.

**Bruce Lee V1** predates the rules. No clip exceeds 10 seconds, but five
source videos carry multiple clips — one of them twelve. Rule 3 is not met.

**MrBeast V12** predates the rules and misses both rule 1 and rule 3: four
source videos carry the whole film and fifteen clips run past 10 seconds, the
longest 22.6s. The owner's own recorded note on this cut was *"I'd rather
just watch the original interview, which means your video is useless."*

## If these are going to be re-cut

Aniston needs nothing. Bruce Lee needs more distinct sources, not shorter
cuts. MrBeast needs both more sources and every clip brought under 10
seconds. The four waiver films are a decision to revisit or not — but the
waiver is the only thing standing between them and rule 3.
